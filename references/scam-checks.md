# UAE job genuineness checklist

Use this to rate each listing ✅ Verified / ⚠️ Caution / ❌ Likely scam. A single strong red flag is enough for ❌. Two or more caution signs without any verified signal → ⚠️. When unsure, choose ⚠️ and say why — let the user decide.

## Why this matters in the UAE
Fake job offers in the UAE commonly ask for "visa fees", "medical test fees" or "deposit for accommodation", and sometimes use real company names and logos. UAE labour rules place recruitment and visa costs on the employer, so any request for money from the candidate is the clearest scam signal.

## ❌ Strong red flags
1. Any payment requested from the candidate (visa, registration, training, medical, uniform, "refundable deposit", processing fee).
2. Contact only through WhatsApp, Telegram or a mobile number; no company email.
3. Free email (gmail.com, yahoo.com, hotmail.com, outlook.com) used by someone claiming to be a large company or government entity.
4. Website domain doesn't match the company (e.g. emirates-careers-jobs.com for Emirates Group), or the site is newly made with no other presence.
5. Offer without an interview, "selected" before applying, or guaranteed visa + job for anyone.
6. Salary far above market with little or no experience required (e.g. AED 15,000+ for a data-entry or helper role).
7. Asks for passport copy, Emirates ID or bank details before any interview.

## ⚠️ Caution signs
- Company name hidden ("confidential client") and the agency doesn't show a MOHRE licence.
- Description is vague, copy-pasted, full of spelling errors, or has no requirements.
- Appears only on classifieds/social media, not on any proper job board or careers page.
- Same post re-listed for many months.
- Recruiter's LinkedIn profile is new, has few connections, or doesn't list the company.
- Job location says "UAE" only with no city, and no office address anywhere.

## ✅ Verified signals
- The same job appears on the company's own careers page (strongest signal).
- Posted by the company's official LinkedIn page, or by a well-known licensed recruiter that names the client.
- Company has a working website, a LinkedIn page with real employees, and a UAE address.
- Corporate email domain matches the company website.
- Clear job title, responsibilities, requirements and application process.

## Quick checks you can actually run
- Search `"<company name>" UAE` — does a real website / LinkedIn page come up?
- Search `"<company name>" careers` — is the job listed there?
- Search `"<company name>" scam` or `"<phone number>"` / `"<email>"` — are there warnings?
- For recruitment agencies, check whether a MOHRE licence is mentioned.

Keep these checks proportionate: run them for listings that aren't obviously from a known employer's official page. A job posted on emirates.com/careers needs no further checking.
